Crack width → prestress left → stiffness and deflection

Set the crack width as if you had just measured it at midspan of a prestressed concrete bridge girder. Three finite-element models turn that width into the remaining prestress η, the stiffness of every element along the span, and the deflection under the design truck, with the band that the image resolution allows.

How this page works

  • Your browser runs no finite-element analysis. scripts/build_demo_data.py solved every model in advance over a dense range of η, and the page interpolates linearly between those solved points:
  • η is the effective prestress as a fraction of the design value. The width is the predicted crack width at midspan under the full dead load (Eurocode 2, 7.3.4); the stiffness of every element follows its own moment (7.4.3) and is iterated with a secant loop, which only matters on the indeterminate grillage.
  • Band: η solved again at w + u and w − u, with u = 2·GSD (the proposal's method). The report's Monte Carlo 5–95 % band lies inside it.
  • Refused readings: the crack-measurement step refuses widths below 3 px. A refused reading still tells something: the crack is narrower than 3 px, so η cannot be below the value shown.
  • Deflection: midspan deflection from the design truck alone (HS20-44 × 1.30, with impact), compared with L/800 = 30.6 mm.
  • Scope: synthetic crack images and a stand-in AASHTO Type IV girder bridge (24.5 m, five girders). This demonstrates the computational chain and its resolution limits, not a field assessment or a capacity check.
